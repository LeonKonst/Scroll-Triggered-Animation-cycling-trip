Our Cycling Trip to Møns Klint

The idea

Over the last year, we lived in Denmark, and we wanted to explore the country. Unfortunately, coming from Greece a country with a wide variety of landscapes, Denmark couldn't really compete. We both love cycling, so we thought, why not cycle to one of the most famous natural attractions in Denmark?

Now, we know that Denmark is not known for its mountains or high peaks, but it has its own charm. Greece may have diverse landscapes, but Denmark stands out for its exceptional cycling infrastructure. The entire country is accessible by bike, making it easy to explore even remote areas. So, we decided to take advantage of this and cycle to one of Denmark's most famous natural attractions: Møns Klint.

Planning the trip

Of course, we didn't want to just cycle to Møns Klint and back in one day, as it would be too tiring and not enjoyable. So, in 3 days we should reach Møns Klint and come back to Copenhagen as next day we both had to be adults. Also we needed a day before the trip to prepare our bikes and relax as we weren't in the best shape and not really experienced with bike packing. That's the main reason we chose not to camp but stay in accomodations along the way.

Unexpectedly, we found out that the 29th of May is a public holiday in Denmark, which was perfect for our trip. We could take the 30th of May off, which was a Friday, and have a long weekend to cycle to Møns Klint and back. So, we planned our trip for the 30th of May to the 1st of June. We booked all the accomodations in advance, planned all our stops and just waitied for the day to come!

Rather than just waiting, we started researching the route and the places we would visit. After a long research, we agreed that we can do a total of 200 km in 3 days, which means we had to cycle around 70 km per day. Also, assuming an avarage speed of 20 km/h, we would need around 3.5 hours of cycling each day. We also had to consider the time for breaks, food and sightseeing, so we planned to have a stop every 20 km for a break and a snack. This way we could enjoy the ride and not get too tired, as this was our first cycling trip. Thankfully, there was always something interesting every 20 km, so we could take a break and enjoy the scenery.

We chose to skip the first 20 km from Copenhagen to Ishøj, as the route is not very pleasant and we could take the train to get there. The first day we would cycle from Ishøj to Rødvig, which is about 60 km. The second day we would cycle from Rødvig to Stubberup, which is about 70 km. The last day we would cycle from Stubberup to Møns Klint and then to Vordingborg, which is about 70 km and then take the train back to Copenhagen. Solid plan, right?

As this was our first trip, we haven't invested yet for bike packing gear, so we had to fit everything in our backpacks. We had to be very careful with what we would take with us, as we didn't want to carry too much weight. We packed the essentials: clothes, food, water, tools, and a first aid kit. You can find all the spots we visited and ate in the Google Map.

Day 1

The day has come! We took the train to Ishøj and started our trip. The weather was Danish, kind of sunny and always ready to give a rainy surprise. We started almost instantly with a break, as we our first stop was Oscar Under The Bridge. Oscar is part of a series of wooden sculptures made by the Danish artist Thomas Dambo. People wrongfully thint that these sculptures are only located in denmark, but they are actually all over the world. You can check the actual locations of the sculptures in the official map.

Our trip continues to Køge, where we will have our first break for a snack. This part of the route was not that interesting. There was a continuum of houses, that gave you the sense that you haven't yet escaped from the city. We arrived to Køge, stopped at a known bakery chain, ate a patry, drank a coffee and after a 20 minute break we started again cycling to Stevns Klint.

Our next stop was Stevns Klint, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the wind started to pick up. We visited the lighthouse, which is free to enter, and we climbed to the top. The view was amazing, and we could see the cliffs and the sea from above.

Unfortunately, The final 10km were a bit challenging, as the wind was against us and we were a little bit tired and hungry. Thankfully, the accomodation felt like home as it was a fully equipped apartment with a kitchen, a living room and a bedroom. We left all our stuff, removed our cycling clothes, had a shower and started looking for a place to eat. We found a nice restaurant called Lingterna, which was an all you can eat buffet. The restaurant included a salad bar, a lot of sushi options (including vegan options), and a variety of ice creams, all included in the price! After filling our stomachs, we returned to our apartment and played some board games that were available there. We didn't need more than a minute to fall asleep after we laid down on the bed. You can find the route of the first day in the Strava activity.

Day 2

Morning came, and we were ready for the second day of our trip. We woke up, packed our stuff and started cycling to Faxe Ladeplads. The route was mostly through small villages and fields, but it was really busy with cars. The reason was revealed to us later in our day. We stopped at SuperBrugsen, a local supermarket, to buy some snacks and coffee for our break and back on the road. Next stop: Præstø. Same as before, the route was through small villages and full of cars.

Few hundrend meters before we reached Præstø, the car density increased significantly. We locked our bikes and started walking towards the city center where a festival was taking place. The festival was peculiar in nature, as it was a celebration of "France Spring". I tried to find more information about the origin of this festival, and all I found was something in between the lines of "Paris themed events at 1950 was the starting point of the festival". We took advantage of the festival and had a longer brake than usual, as there were a lot of things to see and do.

It was about time to continue our trip, and next stop was the Queen Alexandrine bridge, the bridge connecting Zealand and Møn. The bridge is a beautiful sight. Reaching the highest point, we were able to see the sea and the surrounding. The time was around 16:00, and we were getting hungry. We had to find a place to eat, and we were lucky enough to find a restaurant called Saftig Café & Snackbar. We ordered two burgers, one with meat and one vegan, and one big portion of fries. The portions was as described, big! I really enjoyed the vegan burger, so I bought one more to take with me for dinner as we would stay at a place away from any restaurants.

The initial plan was to just cycle to the place we would stay, sleep and then cycle to Møns Klint the next day. But the weather forecast for the next day was not looking good, and we didn't want to cycle in the rain. So, we decided to cycle to Møns Klint that day and then cycle back to the room we rented, leaving only the last day for the return trip. This meant that we had to cycle in total close to 100km that day, which was a bit too much for us.

So, we cycled to our accomodation, checked in left most of our stuff and cycled to Møns Klint. The last 5 km were mudroad so we chose to hike instead of cycling. Hiked for some time, got lost, then found the right path and finally the final stairs to Møns Klint beach. And after 419 steps, we reached the beach. Nothing significant to be fair, the smell was bad and the view was... ok. But as I have already mentioned, it is hard to be surprised by a beach when you come from Greece.

Ticking the box of visiting Møns Klint, we started cycling back to our room. The route was mostly downhill, so we were able to cycle faster than before. We arrived at our room, took a shower and stayed at the yard in the middle of the building complex. The accomodation (Camønogaarden) was really cozy, and had a "trust shop" where you could buy drinks and snacks and just leave the money there. After eating and chatting for a while, we went to our room and slept in record time. You can find the route of the second day in the Strava activity.

Day 3

With our "todo" list for the trip complete, we were ready for the last day of our trip. We packed our stuff and started cycling back to Vordingborg, where we would take the train back to Copenhagen. On our way back, we stopped at Stege to eat at David´s. Again, the food was really good, significantly cheaper than in Copenhagen and the portions were big. Stege surprised us with its food quality! Then back again on the road, cycling through the bridge of Queen Alexandrine, and then to Vordingborg.

Last stop for an ice cream at CHOCO Isbar and then to the train station. We took the train back to Copenhagen, and the feeling when we arrived home was extremely satisfying. We had cycled 200 km in 3 days, visited Møns Klint, and now we were back home, tired, hungry buuut we will sleep in our own bed!

Conclusions

Overall, the trip was a great experience. We cycled 200 km in 3 days, visited Møns Klint, and we learned a lot about bike packing, and we are already planning our next trip. If I had to do it again, I would probably do the exact same route, rent the same accomodations, and eat at the same places!
